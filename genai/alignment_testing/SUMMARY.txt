    THE SOLUTION: FORCED ALIGNMENT
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

How it works:

1. Parent reads: "You are Adalyn..."
                  ↓
2. Acoustic Model predicts: [Phoneme probabilities per frame]
                  ↓
3. Script provides: ["You"=[Y,UW], "are"=[AA,R], ...]
                  ↓
4. Alignment matches: Audio phonemes → Script phonemes
                  ↓
5. Output: word_index=1 (currently saying "are")


Key differences:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 Boundary Detection  |  Forced Alignment      │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Knows script?        NO              |       YES             │
│ Output type?         Timestamp       |   Word index          │
│ Recovers errors?     NO              |       YES             │
│ Latency?             750ms           |     <50ms             │
│ Training data?       LibriSpeech     |  LibriSpeech +        │
│                                      |  Parent reading       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    WHY WE WERE STRUGGLING
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

• Boundary detection CAN'T solve word tracking
• It's like asking "where are the doors?" when you need "which room am I in?"
• No amount of tuning will fix a fundamental architecture mismatch
